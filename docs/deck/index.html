02 The gap

One window, two opinions.

What the model says against what the book asks. The distance between them is the trade, and it is the only thing this deck is about.

ETH-0-02SEP26-0330/tUSDC, 318 seconds before expiry. Real figures from the transaction in slide 09, not an illustration.

03 The problem

The number is knowable. Nobody computes it.

Given where the price sits relative to the window's open, how long is left, and how volatile the underlying is, the probability of closing above the open is a calculation.

Yet the book is quoted by people eyeballing a chart. And when a window settles, there is no way to tell whether a quote was skilful or lucky, because nothing was written down beforehand. Every track record in prediction markets is a claim made after the fact.

01

Resting liquidity. Nobody trades an empty book. The constraint is capital: a maker normally needs inventory on both sides.

02

Prices worth trusting. If the quote is a guess, the spread is compensation for not knowing rather than for taking risk.

03

Settlement worth trusting. Automatic settlement is only as convincing as a trader's ability to check it independently.

Three things a new venue needs.

04 The insight

Two voices, one number.

Derive the probability from the price process, then move it with the news. Whenever the result disagrees with the book by more than fees, there is a trade.

The prior, from the price process

A closed-form barrier probability. σ is measured from live ticks, not assumed; τ is the fraction of the window still open.

The posterior, from the news

Log-odds is the right space: evidence is additive there, so headlines compound without any one running the probability to 0 or 1. Each decays by age, source credibility, and time left.

The news layer is excluded from the published backtest. A historical scout window cannot be reconstructed without leaking the future, so every number in slides 07 and 08 measures the price-process prior alone.

05 How it works

Four subsystems, one command.

Python reads and models; TypeScript owns every write, because that is where the Bot Kit and markets-sdk own signing, nonces and escrow, and two senders on one key race each other.

01

Derive. The prior from the price process, tilted by scored headlines, refreshed every few seconds per live window.

02

Decide. Take when the posterior clears the touch by more than fees. Otherwise rest a two-sided quote and wait to be hit.

03

Trade. Through the official dreamDEX Bot Kit and @somnia-chain/markets-sdk, against the real CLOB.

04

Commit. Each forecast is written to a VaticrForecastRegistry contract while the window is still open. Append-only.

05

Audit. Every settled window is recomputed from the public oracle feed, compared to the onchain winner, and Brier-scored in the app.

Step 05 is the one that could embarrass us, which is why it ships in the product rather than in a slide. If the model drifts, users see it at the same moment we do.

06 Mint-a-pair

Two resting buys are a complete quote.

On a binary book, Buy YES × Buy NO needs no seller. The pool mints a fresh pair for two opposite-side buyers, so two resting bids make a two-sided market with no inventory and no counterparty maker.

CrossingPathWhat happens
Buy YES × Sell YESdirecttokens ↔ collateral
Buy NO × Sell NOdirecttokens ↔ collateral
Buy YES × Buy NOmint-a-pairthe pool mints a fresh pair, no seller needed
Sell YES × Sell NOburn-a-pairboth positions burn

Why it matters to the venue

The binding constraint on a young book is the capital a maker must hold to quote both sides. This removes it: one small agent can quote every live window at once, carrying only its net imbalance.

Why it matters to us

Vaticr never sells. It quotes around a probability it actually computed, so the spread is compensation for risk rather than for ignorance, and it can afford to sit tighter than a maker who is guessing.

08 Does it work · skill

Skill by fraction of the window elapsed.

The edge sharpens as the window closes.

Less time left means less the price can still do, so the derived prior gets sharper. The edge more than triples between 25% and 75% elapsed.

This is the argument for short windows, and it is why the bot trades the 300-second contracts: +0.3865 over n=597.

10 What we found

Two undocumented facts change what a correct price is.

Building this surfaced six substantive issues in the SDK and docs, each hit in practice and verified against live testnet data. Two are not cosmetic.

01

Settlement resolves against the EMA, not spot. Over eight consecutive settlements mark reproduced the onchain winner 8/8 while spot managed 6/8, disagreeing on exactly the near-the-money windows most worth trading.

02

That feed is a 1-second EMA, so naive volatility reads about 4× too low. Consecutive increments are heavily autocorrelated; the textbook estimator measures the smoothing, not the process. Volatility has to come off spot on a 30-second grid.

03

The opening price is not published anywhere. It is the strike of every window, and it has to be reconstructed from tick history.

04

ec-core cannot be installed. It is marked private inside the Bot Kit monorepo, so every team building outside that repo will vendor it independently.

05

Plus two more, and what worked well. fetchPriceCandles is broken on the testnet feed, and several venues run concurrently on one deployment. The report also records what was good, because feedback that only complains is not useful to the team receiving it.

Submitted as the hackathon's optional SDK and documentation feedback report.

11 Where this goes

Liquidity that can explain itself.

Near term

More windows quoted at once, because zero-inventory quoting scales with compute rather than capital. Session keys so the trading key cannot withdraw. A public calibration feed any trader can check before deciding whether our quotes deserve their trust.

Further out

The forecast registry is a general instrument: any agent can commit a probability before the fact and be scored on it afterwards. A venue where forecasters compete on a public Brier score is better price discovery than one where they compete on confidence.
