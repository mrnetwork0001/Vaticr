02 The problem

The number is knowable. Nobody computes it.

A binary event contract has a closed-form fair value. Given where the price sits relative to the window's open, how long is left, and how volatile the underlying is, the probability of closing above the open is a calculation, not a guess.

Yet the book is quoted by people eyeballing a chart. And when a window settles, there is no way to tell whether any given quote was skilful or lucky, because nothing was written down beforehand. Every track record in prediction markets is a claim made after the fact.

Three things a new venue needs

01

Resting liquidity. An empty book quotes nothing, and nobody trades an empty book. The constraint is capital: a maker normally needs inventory on both sides.

02

Prices worth trusting. If the quote is a guess, the spread is compensation for not knowing rather than for taking risk.

03

Settlement worth trusting. Automatic on-chain settlement is only as convincing as a trader's ability to check it independently.

03 The insight

Two voices, one number.

What the model says and what the book asks are both estimates of the same probability. Whenever they disagree by more than fees, there is a trade. That is the whole strategy, and it is why this deck has exactly two accent colours.

The prior, from the price process

A closed-form GBM barrier probability. Where the window sits relative to its own open, given time remaining and measured volatility.

σ is measured from live ticks, not assumed. τ is the fraction of the window still open.

The posterior, from the news

Live headlines are scored for directional impact on BTC and ETH, then folded in as log-likelihood ratios. Log-odds is the right space for this: evidence is additive there, so a second headline strengthens the first without either one being able to run the probability to 0 or 1.

Each contribution decays by the headline's age, its source's credibility, and how much of the window is left. Near expiry, news stops mattering and the price process takes over.

The evidence layer is excluded from the published backtest. A historical scout window cannot be reconstructed without leaking the future, so every number in section 06 measures the price-process prior alone. The news layer can only be judged live.

04 How it works

Four subsystems, one command.

Python reads and models; TypeScript owns every write, because that is where the Bot Kit and markets-sdk own signing, nonces and escrow, and two senders on one key race each other.

01

Derive. The prior from the price process, tilted by scored headlines, refreshed every few seconds per live window.

02

Decide. Take when the posterior clears the touch by more than fees. Otherwise rest a two-sided quote and wait to be hit.

03

Trade. Through the official dreamDEX Bot Kit and @somnia-chain/markets-sdk, against the real CLOB. Post-only unless crossing is intended.

04

Commit. Each forecast is written to a VaticrForecastRegistry contract while the window is still open. Append-only, one row per market.

05

Audit. Every settled window is recomputed from the public oracle feed and compared to the on-chain winner, then Brier-scored against a coin flip in the app.

Step 05 is the one that could embarrass us, which is exactly why it ships in the product rather than in a slide. If the model drifts, the calibration view shows it to every user at the same time it shows us.

05 Mint-a-pair

Two resting buys are a complete quote.

On a binary book, Buy YES × Buy NO needs no seller. The pool mints a fresh pair for two opposite-side buyers. So a resting BUY_YES @ p−δ plus BUY_NO @ (1−p)−δ is a two-sided market made with no inventory and no counterparty maker.

CrossingPathWhat happens
Buy YES × Sell YESdirecttokens ↔ collateral
Buy NO × Sell NOdirecttokens ↔ collateral
Buy YES × Buy NOmint-a-pairthe pool mints a fresh pair, no seller needed
Sell YES × Sell NOburn-a-pairboth positions burn

Why it matters to the venue

The binding constraint on a young order book is the capital a maker must hold to quote both sides. Mint-a-pair removes it. One small agent can quote every live window at once, and the only risk it carries is its net imbalance, not a full inventory.

Why it matters to us

Vaticr never sells. It quotes around a probability it actually computed, so the spread is compensation for risk rather than for ignorance, and it can afford to sit tighter than a maker who is guessing.

06 Does it work

900 forecasts, scored against a coin flip.

300 settled windows the model never saw, three decision points each, on 32,413 real ticks per asset. Lookahead-free: volatility and level at every decision point use only ticks at or before that instant, asserted on all 900 cases and re-run against a physically truncated history on 120 of them, all 120 matching.

Reliability. A forecast of 0.75 should be right about three times in four, and it is. The line tracks the diagonal at every bucket, which is what separates a calibrated model from one that is merely confident.

The edge sharpens as the window closes, because less time means less the price can still do. This is the argument for short windows: the 300-second contracts the bot actually trades score +0.3865 over n=597.

08 What we found

Two undocumented facts change what a correct price is.

Building this surfaced six substantive issues in the SDK and docs, each hit in practice and verified against live testnet data. Two of them are not cosmetic: they silently make a careful implementation wrong.

01

Settlement resolves against the EMA, not spot. Over the eight most recent settlements, mark reproduced the on-chain winner 8/8 while spot managed 6/8 - disagreeing on exactly the near-the-money windows most worth trading. Price off the wrong series and you are confidently wrong where it costs most.

02

That feed is a 1-second EMA, so naive volatility reads about 4× too low. Consecutive increments are heavily autocorrelated; the textbook estimator measures the smoothing rather than the process. Volatility has to be estimated from spot on a 30-second grid.

03

The opening price is not published anywhere. It is the strike of every window - the single number the contract's question turns on - and it has to be reconstructed from the tick history.

04

ec-core cannot be installed. It is marked private inside the Bot Kit monorepo, so every team building outside that repo will vendor it independently. Publishing it would be a small release with a large effect.

05

Plus two more, and what worked well. fetchPriceCandles is broken on the testnet feed, and several venues run concurrently on one deployment. The full report also records what was good, because feedback that only complains is not useful to the team receiving it.

Submitted as the hackathon's optional SDK and documentation feedback report, with measurements and suggested fixes for each finding.

09 Where this goes

Liquidity that can explain itself.

Near term

More windows quoted at once, because zero-inventory quoting scales with compute rather than capital. Session keys so the trading key cannot withdraw. A public calibration feed any trader can check before deciding whether our quotes deserve their trust.
